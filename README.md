# cs350RaspPi
A  continuous circuit build to a breadboard thermostat.


    Summarize the project and what problem it was solving.
        This project was a slow buildup from a basic circuit cmprised of an LED and resistor, to a fully functional thermostat with multiple components. The problem this circuit was solving was making a circuit that could utilize incoming data from a sensor and acting off of its values. This was a basic FSM that would change from states of "off. heat, and cool" depending on a set temperature value. It also had 2 LEDS as a visual confirmation of what stat the thermostat would be in at any given time.
    What did you do particularly well?
        I think I performed particularly well in the coding section of this project. In previous modules, I was still trying to understand how the packages worked with states and transitions, and in this final I was able to code the methods properly and according to best practices. I also think working with the hardware came pretty natural, even though your are following a procedure to build. 
    Where could you improve?
        As always diagrams for me are my nemesis. I can visually think how the states work, but for some reason when putting it to paper on a flowing diagram, I begin to confuse myself. The only way I know how to get better is to continously work on them and always diagram before I touch the keyboard.
    What tools and/or resources are you adding to your support network?
        Peopel are always a good resource for me and definitely package documentation and manuals on components are the basis of my support. If I cannot figure out a specific meaning then Google is always a good resource and AI tooling after that to help explain it in terms that I can understand.

    How did you make this project maintainable, readable, and adaptable?
        Working with circuits will be a huge help in my other projects, personal, educational, and hopefully professional. Coding is always going to be a bonus at this point, but understanding the concepts of electrical circuits will be the biggest gain. Researching tactics and techniques are also invaluable, especially with more companies becoming more dependent on AI, I believe at some point we will need to go back to the old fashioned way of figuring things out on our own, which will require lots of reading of docs, schematics, and manuals.
        I was able to keep this project maintainable by implementing in small sequences, always writing a few lines and testing the outcome. This is the same for the physical comnponents with testing a component to make sure there is power and understanding the basics of OHM's Law. Readability and adaptability were inherent with relevent naming conventions and making sure the classes that were written to be read in a natural flow, especially with the methods involved.
